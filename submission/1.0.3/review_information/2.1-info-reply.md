## Paste into the Resolution Center reply

Thank you for the review. Responses to each item:

**1. Screen recording**

Attached — captured on a physical [iPhone model], iOS [version]. It begins at
cold launch and walks the typical user flow: the location-introduction screen
(showing the "Use Union Square instead" option — location is optional), the
iOS location permission prompt (the app's only sensitive-data prompt), the
Spots map and venue list, search ("Housing Works"), a venue detail page
showing claim-level workability evidence (each Wi-Fi, outlet, laptop-policy,
and noise claim with its source, confidence, and observation date) and the
photo gallery with attribution, the Work Fit filters, saving a venue to the
Saved tab (stored on-device only), and the You tab with the full "How scoring
works" methodology plus privacy, terms, and support links.

The app is fully accountless: there is no account registration, login, or
account deletion; no paid content, purchases, or subscriptions; and no
user-generated content. Those flows do not appear in the recording because
they do not exist in the app.

**2. Devices and operating systems tested**

- [iPhone model], iOS [version] (physical device) — full manual pass of this
  exact build via TestFlight
- iPhone 17 Pro Max, iOS 26 (Simulator, Release configuration) — automated
  reviewer-flow test suite against the production backend
- iPad Pro 13-inch (M5), iOS 26 (Simulator, Release configuration) — same
  suite, iPhone-app compatibility

**3. Functions and target audience**

BrewDesk helps remote workers, freelancers, and students choose where to work
from — cafés, libraries, and parks. General-purpose maps show star ratings,
not whether a venue is actually workable. BrewDesk shows claim-level evidence:
explicit laptop policy (including time-limited or laptop-discouraged venues,
shown openly), seating, Wi-Fi, outlets, and noise — each claim with its
source, its confidence, and the date it was observed — plus a transparent
Work Fit score whose complete methodology is published inside the app.
Unknown values are shown as "unknown," never guessed.

**4. Setup and access**

No setup is required: no account, no login credentials, no sample files.
Launch the app; on the location-introduction screen either allow location or
choose "Use Union Square instead" — every feature works without granting
location. The optional Google Maps saved-places import parses the user's
Takeout file entirely on-device; nothing is uploaded.

**5. External services**

- Our own backend API ("Venue Engine"), hosted on Vercel:
  https://venuekit-ashen.vercel.app (public health check: /v1/health)
- Apple MapKit for maps
- OpenStreetMap for baseline venue data (ODbL, attribution shown in the app)
- Google Places API (Photos) for venue photos, proxied through our backend
  and displayed with attribution
- Venue workability research is produced offline in our data pipeline using
  AI-assisted research (Anthropic's Claude) with human curation; every such
  claim is labeled with its source and confidence in the app. The app itself
  makes no calls to AI services.
- No authentication service, payment processor, advertising, or analytics SDKs.

**6. Regional differences**

The app's features are identical in all regions. Only venue-data depth varies,
and the app labels it: New York City is fully researched (2,700+ venues);
50 US metro areas have baseline coverage derived from OpenStreetMap; other
areas show baseline or limited data with an in-app banner stating the
coverage level.

**7. Regulated industry / protected material**

BrewDesk does not operate in a regulated industry. Third-party material is
limited to OpenStreetMap data (used under the ODbL with attribution) and
venue photos retrieved through Google's licensed Places API with attribution
displayed.
