Hello App Review,

Thank you for the review. Everything requested is below. The screen recording is attached.

1. Screen recording — attached. Recorded on a physical iPhone running the current iOS release. It starts at app launch and shows: the location introduction and the system location prompt (the app's only permission request), browsing the Spots map and shelf, the Work Fit filter menu, search, a venue detail with its evidence-backed Workability claims and photo attribution, Directions hand-off to Maps, saving a venue, the Saved tab and the optional import sheet, and the You tab with the published scoring methodology and privacy policy. The app has no account registration, login, or deletion flows, no paid content, purchases, or subscriptions, and no user-generated content, reporting, or blocking in this build, so those do not appear.

2. Devices and operating systems tested — Physical: iPhone 15 Pro on iOS [VERSION], via TestFlight builds 1 through 18 (the submitted build is 18). Automated Release-configuration UI test suites, including a scripted reviewer-flow simulation against the production data service, on iPhone 17 Pro Max and iPad Pro 13-inch (M5) simulators running iOS 26. Minimum supported version is iOS 17.0.

3. What the app does and for whom — BrewDesk helps remote and hybrid workers, students, and freelancers find a place to work from: cafés, libraries, parks, and coworking spaces. The problem: general map apps rate a café for coffee, not for whether you can open a laptop there for three hours. BrewDesk evaluates laptop policy (including venues that limit or discourage laptops, shown openly), seating, Wi-Fi, outlets, and noise, and shows every claim with its source, confidence, and last-updated date. Unknowns are labeled as unknown rather than guessed. The scoring formula is published inside the app (You → How scoring works). It is free, has no accounts, no ads, no purchases, and collects no data.

4. Setup and access — No login, credentials, or sample files are needed. Launch → on the location introduction either allow location or choose "Use Union Square instead" → the Spots tab shows the map and a draggable list. Tap the search field and search "Housing Works", open Housing Works Bookstore Cafe, and scroll to Workability to see per-claim evidence. Tap Save to add it to the Saved tab. Open the You tab for "How scoring works", Privacy Policy, Terms, Support, and OpenStreetMap attribution. The Saved tab's Import button accepts an optional Google Takeout saved-places file, parsed on the device and never uploaded; it is not required to review the app.

5. External services — (a) Our own venue data service, hosted on Vercel at https://venuekit-ashen.vercel.app (public health check: https://venuekit-ashen.vercel.app/v1/health), which serves the curated dataset; (b) OpenStreetMap data (ODbL, attributed in the You tab) for baseline venue coverage, including an Overpass API lookup performed by our server; (c) Google Places photos, fetched through our server and displayed with attribution; (d) Apple MapKit and Core Location for the map and location; (e) Apple Maps for the Directions hand-off. The app makes no calls to authentication, payment, advertising, analytics, crash-reporting, or AI services. Our venue dataset is researched offline by our team from public web sources; that research is not performed by the app.

6. Regional differences — Features are identical in every territory. Data depth varies: New York City is fully researched; 50 other US metro areas have baseline coverage from OpenStreetMap, and the app shows a banner saying so; elsewhere the app performs a live OpenStreetMap lookup or shows an explained empty state. Without location access the app shows the New York City dataset, so it can be fully reviewed from anywhere.

7. Regulated industry / protected material — BrewDesk is not in a regulated industry. Third-party material is limited to OpenStreetMap data used under the ODbL with the required attribution, and Google Places photos displayed via Google's API with the required attribution. Venue names, addresses, and hours are factual data. No other third-party content is included.

We have also added this information to the App Review Information notes for future submissions.
